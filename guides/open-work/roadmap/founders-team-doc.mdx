## Founders Who Live In The Future.

Orbiter.io is built by three media technologists who live a few years ahead of their industry, and who have spent twenty-five years proving it. They were cutting digital video in the dial-up era. One of them took delivery of the first RED Digital Cinema Camera ever shipped. Two of them were the first two people to join Frame.io after its founders, led its go-to-market from private beta all the way through the \$1.275 billion Adobe acquisition, and picked up a Primetime Emmy along the way. They filmed BLACKPINK and Doja Cat in VR for Meta before most studios owned a headset. Now they are building the product for the asset their entire world runs on, the one no tool has ever managed: the professional relationship graph.

Orbiter.io was founded by all three of them together. Three co-founders, three clean lanes: Josh Diamond as CEO, Jason Diamond as Chief Product Officer, and Mark L. Pederson as CTO. The bios below come in two parts only because two of the founders share a single story, not because anyone came first.

### Josh and Jason Diamond, Co-Founders

Josh and Jason Diamond are identical twins who have been creative partners their entire lives. "We were branded at birth," as they put it. They became editors in the mid-1990s, when digital video was a bet most of the industry had not yet placed, and grew that bet into The Diamond Bros, an Emmy-winning New York production company with a client list that reads like the industry itself: Disney, Sony PlayStation, Meta, HBO, Samsung, Hasbro, Sesame Workshop.

The chapter that matters most here is Frame.io. In July 2014, eight months before the public launch, Jason introduced Frame.io's private beta to the professional camera world on REDUSER, signing off as The Diamond Bros / Frame.io. At that moment the company was founders Emery Wells and John Traver plus the twins: Josh and Jason were the first two team members outside the founders. They led go-to-market from that day forward. The early narrative, the launch story, the films and tutorials, the evangelism that made a review tool feel inevitable to working editors and colorists: that was the Diamonds. And they stayed on the ride, through the Accel seed, the FirstMark rounds, the \$50 million Insight Partners Series C, and the \$1.275 billion Adobe acquisition in 2021. Frame.io co-founder John Traver is now an investor in Orbiter.io, which says everything about how the founding team remembers those years.

They share one resume, but not one job. Jason tells the story. Josh sells it.

**Josh Diamond, Co-Founder and CEO.** Josh is the commercial engine. A director and cinematographer with more than twenty years of winning work from the most demanding brands in entertainment and then delivering it, he is described in his own bio as a realist and a dreamer, raised on Star Wars and Kiss. That mix is exactly what shows up in a sales conversation: spectacle and imagination up front, execution underneath. His Frame.io role was literally titled Special Forces, and it fit. He turned a creative community's trust into a growth engine once, watched it end in a ten-figure exit, and is now running the same motion as CEO with his own name on the door.

**Jason Diamond, Co-Founder and Chief Product Officer.** Jason is the storyteller and the product mind. He was an executive producer of "The Magical Wand Chase: A Sesame Street Special," winner of the 2018 Primetime Emmy for Outstanding Children's Program. The siliconvalley.video conference introduced him as "the rare mix of filmmaker and technologist who can talk about story and deep creative vision with the same fluency he uses to discuss data pipelines and custom workflows," and the record backs it up: custom hardware and software workflow tools for production and post, productions ranging from intimate documentaries to massive LED stage builds, SXSW VR filmmaking mentor, host of fxguide's vfx show podcast. At Frame.io he was the voice that explained a brand new category to a skeptical professional audience. At Orbiter.io he shapes the product until relationship intelligence feels just as obvious.

### Mark L. Pederson, Co-Founder and CTO

Mark has spent thirty years building the technical future of film and television, usually before the industry agreed there was one. He co-founded Offhollywood in Brooklyn in 2003 as a digital cinema company when digital cinema was still a fighting phrase, and built it into a RED-ecosystem powerhouse that designed and manufactured its own camera hardware: the OMOD and HotLink camera communication and metadata modules, used on major productions such as Narcos and Netflix's The Dark Crystal. He was, famously, first in line for RED twice. He took delivery of the very first RED camera shipped in 2007, and when the RED EPIC followed in 2010, the first of those went to him too: serial number 006, a camera he had put his hand up for years before it existed.

Offhollywood's story ends the way conviction stories should: acquired, twice. Light Iron bought its SoHo post-production operations in 2013 to enter the New York market, and Light Iron itself became part of Panavision. The Vitec Group then acquired the Offhollywood Digital hardware and rental business in 2016, folding it into its Creative Solutions division alongside Teradek and SmallHD.

His next act was AI before AI was in every deck. In 2018 he co-founded Color Intelligence with color scientist Dado Valentic, serving as Co-Founder | COO, and in 2020 they launched Colourlab Ai: an AI color grading system used across film and television that automates the repetitive craft of shot matching while keeping the colorist in creative control.

At Orbiter.io, Mark is the architect of the intelligence layer: a context graph fused with vector search, so the system can both reason over relationships and recall meaning from everything around them. In his words: "The graph gives you reasoning; the vector DB gives you recall. Separated, each stays excellent at its job. Linked, they compound."

## Why this is the team to build Orbiter.io

Investors describe founder-market fit as an earned secret plus lived obsession. Here is what that looks like in the specific.

**They are the customer.** Orbiter.io's first market is media and entertainment, an industry with no org chart, where careers are made by who remembers you and who takes your call. The founders have lived in that economy for thirty years, on both sides of the introduction.

**They have run this exact play before.** Frame.io won by embedding into how creative professionals already worked and spreading through their networks. The two people who led that go-to-market are Orbiter.io's CEO and CPO. The lesson they earned was not that video review is valuable. It was how tools spread through relationship-driven industries. That is the secret Orbiter.io is built on.

**The technical bet has a track record.** Mark has shipped ahead-of-the-market infrastructure three times: digital cinema services in 2003, camera hardware that sold through two acquisitions, and AI color grading in 2020. Orbiter.io's graph-plus-vector architecture follows the same pattern he has repeated for decades: find the workflow everyone suffers through, automate the repetitive layer, keep the human in control.

**Distribution is already warm.** Pilot commitments from a top talent agency, a premium network, a marquee industry festival, an entertainment AI community, and a leading technical university (names available in diligence). An invite-only waitlist past 10,000. And the founders' own networks, built over thirty years in exactly the market being sold to, are the first proof of product.

**The reference check is already on the cap table.** Frame.io co-founder John Traver backs Orbiter.io, alongside Kyle Jackson and Bilawal Sidhu. Backchannel diligence on the Frame.io story ends with a person who chose to invest.

**The partnership risk is priced in at zero.** Co-founder conflict is one of the top reasons startups die. Two of these founders have been partners since birth and describe their working dynamic as a shared creative consciousness. All three have worked the same RED, Meta, and HBO orbits for fifteen years. The lanes are clean: Josh runs the company, Jason runs the product, Mark runs the technology.

## Katelyn Gallanty, Fractional CFO (via Grant Drive Group)

Orbiter.io's fundraises are managed by Katelyn Gallanty as fractional CFO through Grant Drive Group, the capital advisory firm she founded.

Katelyn's career has run along the exact seam this document is written for: what founders need to scale, and what investors need to see before they deploy. At Citi she was selected to lead a product innovation initiative inside Citi Ventures, taking a cross-border payments product with embedded FX hedging from idea to live, globally commercialized product in eighteen months. Wells Fargo recruited her to run commercialization for its entire FX payments unit across eight client segments. Citi then brought her back to build something new: a national private banking practice dedicated to founders and entrepreneurs in fintech, AI, and life sciences on the path to IPO. She built that practice from zero to 175 clients and \$3 billion in assets under management in two and a half years, becoming a nationally ranked private banker.

Grant Drive Group is the synthesis of all of it: \$25MM\+ raised across 35\+ companies, a network of 1,500\+ accredited investors and qualified purchasers, and a working style built on facing the hard questions weeks before an investor asks them. She holds FINRA Series 6, 7, 22, 31, 63, 66, 82, and 99 licenses, with the Series 79 in progress. For Orbiter.io this means institutional-grade financial discipline from day one: models an investment committee can pull apart, a data room that answers in hours, and a finance partner who has personally sat on the capital side of the table.

GDG is named for Katelyn's grandfather, who went from the mailroom to the boardroom without a college degree. That is the standard of grit she holds founders to, and the one she signed up for here.

- **Consistency sweep before sending.** Jason's CPO title is not stated anywhere public; his LinkedIn headline reads "Co-Founder Orbiter.io | Partner | Director." Investors cross-check the data room against LinkedIn, and team slides are getting more scrutiny, not less (DocSend measured VCs spending 40% more time on seed team slides in 2024 vs 2023). Align LinkedIn headlines with the titles used here before the doc goes out.
- **Numbers discipline.** This doc uses \$1.275 billion everywhere. Press rounds it to \$1.3B; let the reader do the rounding, not us.
- **RED firsts sourcing.** The first shipped RED EPIC (serial 006, December 2010) has public receipts linked in the press footer. The very first RED camera in 2007 is founder-sourced; if a public receipt exists (REDUSER archives are the likely place), link it next to the claim before the doc ships.
- **"First two non-founding members" sourcing.** Stated on the founders' own authority. The July 2014 REDUSER post is the public receipt, and John Traver on the cap table settles any backchannel. Keep the receipt linked wherever the claim appears.
- **Colourlab phrasing.** Press describes Steve Bayes as angel investor and advisor, not co-founder, and Colourlab's current founders page lists only Dado Valentic. The safe form used here: Mark co-founded Color Intelligence (maker of Colourlab Ai) with Dado Valentic, serving as COO.
- **Offhollywood exits phrasing.** Two separate transactions: post operations to Light Iron (2013, Light Iron later acquired by Panavision) and the hardware/rental assets to Vitec (2016). Never compress this to "Panavision acquired Offhollywood."
- **Josh's "Special Forces" title.** Verifiable via Crunchbase and a genuinely great detail. Keep unless counsel prefers formal titles only.
